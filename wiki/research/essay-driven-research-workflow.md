## Answer

**Inference:** Treat one 1,500–2,000-word essay as one turn. Accept a paste, local file, or blog URL; preserve its text and provenance as evidence, but never as authority for external facts. Keep the author’s voice distinct from findings, and do not publish private drafts without explicit authorization. ([Essay-driven research request, 2026](../../sources/essay-driven-research-request/))

**Inference:** Map the thesis, factual claims, assumptions, named entities, explicit questions, and plausible latent questions. Rank a few lines by impact on the argument and uncertainty instead of searching every sentence. Ask the author to select scope when priorities might change intended emphasis, latent questions are ambiguous, or private material may be quoted; otherwise pursue the highest-leverage targets. ([Essay-driven research request, 2026](../../sources/essay-driven-research-request/))

**Inference:** For each target, discover candidates and capture direct primary sources before synthesis. Preserve provenance, separate support from incompatible evidence, and label unresolved conflict as contradiction rather than blending it away. Synthesize around the author’s questions and vocabulary, with warranted caveats. Return a compact answer, durable wiki pages only when useful, and next-cycle material: strengthened claims, challenged assumptions, open questions, useful quotations, and revision or follow-up options. ([Essay-driven research request, 2026](../../sources/essay-driven-research-request/))
