## Overview

TypeSafe AI founder Diogo Almeida introduces "System One Models", a proposed class of models for fast, typed probabilistic decisions inside software, and Jev, the first of them: it reads program state and predefined questions and returns constrained values with probabilities instead of prose. The launch post also reports latency, pricing, and four workflow evaluations. All results are company-reported: TypeSafe's own team built the workflows, the reference answers come from other models rather than independent ground truth, and no architecture, training, or replication details are published. The page shows September 15, 2026, while its metadata says September 21.

## Key points

- Jev takes unstructured program state and predefined questions and returns type-constrained values with probabilities and confidence scores rather than generated prose. (Opening product description; "Frontiers, Old and New" > "Inputs", "Outputs", and "Confidence")
- The company reports end-to-end response times of 70 to 500 milliseconds, input pricing of $0.042 per million tokens, and unmetered output. (Sections "Frontiers, Old and New" > "Cost" and "Speed"; "Evidence / Technical Results", opening list)
- Four company-built workflows compare Jev with external models, using the averaged probabilities of those models as reference answers. (Section "Evidence / Technical Results" > "Workflow evals" and its "Nuance" list)
