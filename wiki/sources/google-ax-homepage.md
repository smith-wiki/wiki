## Overview

The AX homepage presents Google's orchestrator as infrastructure for declaring agent tasks, configuring them from repositories, running them at very large scale, and letting agents build and operate other agents through AX's own resources. It is an undated promotional page aimed at developers. It offers no benchmark for its "billions of tasks" claim, and its production-ready tone conflicts with the [repository's](../google-ax/) warning that AX is pre-stable and still changing. Read it for how the project positions itself, and the repository for how AX works.

## Key points

- Users declare an agentic task and manage it with apply, watch, SSH, suspend, resume, and delete commands. (Hero "Declare an agentic task. AX runs it at scale." and its terminal example)
- The page calls agents a new kind of workload and claims AX scales from one to billions of tasks. (Sections "Why AX", "Agents are a new kind of workload."; "How it works", "Scales up to billions of tasks.")
- AX is meant to let agents create and operate other agents by exposing its resources as tools. (Section "Generative platform")
